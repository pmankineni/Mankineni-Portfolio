The BW → Datasphere move isn’t a new topic for me. It’s the third time I’ve carried a landscape onto a new SAP platform generation.

Current engagement 2025 – now Basler AG

Rebuilding a BW landscape in SAP Datasphere.

Exactly the move you’re evaluating — and I’m in the middle of it right now.

The situation

Reporting runs on SAP BW. Years of modelling have accumulated business logic inside transformations — not in a document, in the code.

What I build

Replication flows off the source systems. Data and transformation flows to re-express the BW logic. Fact and analytic models as the new consumption layer.

Where it actually gets hard

A BW transformation is not a view. Start, end and field routines carry rules with no direct Datasphere equivalent. Deciding what becomes a flow, what becomes SQL, and what has to be redesigned is the real work. The object-to-object mapping is the easy half.

  • SAP Datasphere
  • Replication flows
  • Transformation flows
  • Analytic models
  • SAP BW
  • SAP HANA
Current engagement 2024 – now Mankiewicz

BW-on-HANA — from data model to the number in the workbook.

I don’t hand off at the model boundary. Same pair of hands from extraction through to what the business actually opens on Monday morning.

The situation

Complex data models and flows on BW-on-HANA, feeding business reporting that has to reconcile.

What I build

The models and flows, AMDP and ABAP enrichment where standard transformations run out of road, then Analysis for Office workbooks and SAC dashboards on top.

Where it actually gets hard

Pushdown discipline. AMDP earns its extra complexity only where it replaces row-by-row ABAP with set-based SQL on HANA. Used everywhere, it just moves the maintenance problem — so knowing when not to matters as much as knowing how.

  • BW-on-HANA
  • AMDP
  • ABAP-OO
  • SQLScript
  • Analysis for Office
  • SAP Analytics Cloud
Flagship transformation 2017 – 2022 Fresenius AG

Replacing a 25-year-old reporting solution. Globally.

Global Sales Reporting (GSIS) — five years on one platform, from architecture through to rollout.

The situation

Global sales reporting ran on an ERP-based solution that had been in service for 25 years. Every workaround in it was load-bearing.

What I built

A global BW/4HANA + SAC reporting platform on an LSA++ architecture, and the migration off the legacy solution.

Where it actually gets hard

A global rollout means one model absorbing regional variation without forking — the moment each region gets its own branch, you own a maintenance problem forever. Disciplined LSA++ layering is what makes that survivable at scale.

  • BW/4HANA
  • LSA++
  • SAP Analytics Cloud
  • Global rollout
  • Legacy migration
